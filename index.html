What I Solved

01

Inconsistent Team Performance

I inherited a team of 20 sales reps when stepping into this role and their MoM performance for the last two quarters was wildly inconsistent. They had hit 5M the year prior, facilitated by a new pricing structure that was launched which made the process simplified, which allowed even this inconsistent team to organically hit 5M.

The last person to lead them was pretty content with that as it meant they hit their quotas and had no heat on them, even though everything about it, when looked closely, signaled to a broken down coaching cadence and a reliance on Aggressive marketing initiatives and pricing structures at a time that Charter was trying to capture back lost business and a 56% dip in stock value to deliver results.

Not being one to rely on market conditions or attrition for meeting performance, I immediately began creating standards for how they should handle customers, what the customer journey should like, a more natural discovery rhythm and most importantly a recurring weekly meeting where we'd all, as a group, go over the most voted on objections they encountered or difficult situations they wanted guidance on. (I built a website with a form and voting function to allow them to vote on this and had quick links to a custom performance tracker I made them, too - one which auto reported outcomes in real time, and updated in real time, too -- but I'll talk more about that later).

After some months of targeted growth, I was able to turn this team into one that was a lot more independent, and with better judgement. My goal was never to make a team that had all the answers, though - it was to make one that knew how to respond to feeling immense pressure and to get out of uncertainty in a way that doesn't jeopardize the customer journey.

Graciously, after some months, my day to day life went from answering roughly 50 questions an hour to only being brought in for intervention and skill gaps shortening to where I was only queried just over 10 times an hour.

02

High Customer Churn Within the Quarter

At Charter, and in Insurance, we'd only get paid if the business stayed sold. Within insurance, we'd actually get hit with Chargebacks if they canceled within the time it was written. Honestly, I'm grateful to have spent so many years in Insurance, because it really established what Lifetime Value meant, and how the customer journey needs to be structured and handled in such a way that keeps them feeling confident in their choice to do business with us and renew for many years to come.

I created a training to help them navigate how to handle customers, informed not only by the almost 1400 policies that I wrote personally, but from the Millions of dollars of AP I had to retain annually because it was at risk of leaving for things that had nothing to do with me, and almost always triggered by either the acquisition strategy, or the sales process itself and how it was handled by the rep who initiated the policy. When you are dealing with irate customers who want nothing to do with you as a business, a lot of truth comes to light, and I'm glad for the years of exposure I've had to the dirty dark parts of sales that let me know what never to do when writing my own business.

Ultimately, the activation and retention rate within 3 months was around 72%, after me reinforcing how to speak with customers with the end goal in mind, with expectations established early on, and checking for understanding and changing from pitching to educating a customer on how to make an informed decision (one that conveniently ends with us being the solution) my Activation and 3 month Retention rating went all the way up to 93%.

03

Severe Lack of Time for Admin, Coaching, and Accountability Conversations

Just in case you've never worked at a Fortune 50 company that dominates the market and tens of millions of customers, here's the kind of activity that happened on average for JUST our department.

88,000 Calls Daily
11,000 Per Site
2,000 Per Branch
400 Per Frontline Leader

So there was virtually no down time in between calls, and there's fires to put out virtually every handful of minutes. Not to mention, severe amounts of executive pressure from Site VPs and Directors - the kind that isn't always communicated well, with the hint of what may happen if you don't improve.

Ultimately, we're responsible for ensuring that our performance is always above water, and that we're never the outliers across the site, and then across the organization, because there are hundreds of fires happening in real time at any given moment and no one wants to be the outlier. Pretty stressful, eh?

Well, the short answer is, it requires more than 10 hours of work we do -- I honestly ended up working round 14 hours daily just to stay on top of it, something that many others didn't and found themselves severely behind and constantly missing deliverables.

So, how do you make time? Simple, I find ways to be more proficient at the technology we have and the restrictions we have in place for doing so by mastering Excel and creating dashboards and knowledge bases that are easier to search than company made ones, and streamlining their decision making ability so that I don't become the bottleneck.

I designed a tracker that they could place all their activity into, all they had to do was put the outcome of the call. The Excel Sheet automatically populated the rest and an update and also fed into our other reporting systems so that I didn't have to do any manual reporting on anything, more importantly than that, I coded into it goals which were real and based on where they were MTD, which always put daily goals to be on pace for hitting quota based on where they personally were, and also giving visibility how above they were.

04

Mobile Product Pays the Most, but Activates the Least

To echo off a prior sentiment, we don't get paid unless business sticks for an extended period of time. When it comes to what pays us the most, Mobile, the team had a disgustingly low figure. This ended up being acceptable by everyone because mobile had a unique position, it was the clearest revenue opportunity within the organization and was also one of the most operationally constrained.

Mobile represented one of our highest-value products, but despite strong customer interest and successful sales conversations, activation completion rates were significantly underperforming.

Customers were saying yes. The sales team was creating demand.. but somewhere between the initial purchase decision and completed activation, the customer journey was breaking down. At the time, wireless activation adoption was approximately 37%, creating a significant gap between sold business and realized recurring revenue.

The challenge was that this was not a traditional sales performance issue. The barriers impacting activation existed across multiple parts of the customer journey — including activation processes, technical dependencies, operational workflows, compliance requirements, and customer understanding.

How do we improve an outcome owned by the entire organization while ensuring frontline teams remain accountable for the parts they can influence?

I organized and led a cross-functional task force across Sales, Retention, Activation, Operations, Compliance, Technical Support, and FCC stakeholders.

After months of iterations, biweekly meetings and analyzing the data at each stage to inform if our method was working, Mobile activation ended up going from 37% all the way to 85%.

The total impact of that was an increased revenue production sitewide of over $10M

05

New Hire Ramp Time is Too Long

Anyone who gets paid on revenue production can feel the pain of a team that takes too long to be good at their job and deliver results.

Thankfully, after some time building success, I was able to turn my enablement strategy into a process that was easy to facilitate.

The outcomes I had prove my method, but let me tell you about the format I chose to use.

At Allstate I had made a training platform for new agents who were struggling, too -- and the LMS format I used resonated very well with agents. Now in our hectic work environment, I had to make it bite sized and referenceable to serve as a "Field Guide" for quick reference, and so I began building the same system for my agents and it greatly improved their ability to get a good read of what kind of buyer they had, what ICP profile they fell into, and how to navigate the conversation based on what's important to them and guide them through a solution.

Since my departure from Charter, I've since advised multiple companies across different industries on this process and built enablement systems for them, too. I have deployed over 20 LMSs this year across multiple industries: Telecom Construction, Telecom Sales, Foundation Repair, Window and Door Replacement, Mobile/Video sales, Trailer Sales, Insurance, Cabinets, Real Estate, Medical Device Sales, FinTech SaaS, and many more.
